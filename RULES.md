# Challenge rules

Rules for the Albatroz "Panorama Localisation" challenge at JunctionX Lisbon 2026, from the challenge PDF (`Albatroz Challenge - JunctionX Lisbon 2026.pdf`) and from what the partner told us on site. Where the two differ, the partner's clarification wins.

## The task

- **Input:** a 360° equirectangular panorama, 6080×3040 JPG, daylight, taken no more than 5 m above ground, not edited. The filename carries the capture date and time (`IMG_YYYYMMDD_hhmmss_00_NNN.jpg`). Each panorama is labelled "Urban" or "non-Urban", but the team only learns the label after submitting (see Organiser answers).
- **Region:** mainly Portugal, also Spain and France.
- **Output:** a list of up to 10 locations, each a single `(lat, lng)` pair, sorted by decreasing likelihood.
- **Ground truth is never revealed.** The team must judge whether a proposed location is plausible.

## Scoring (PDF rules 1–12)

1. The list holds at most 10 entries, one `(lat, lng)` pair each.
2. Distance is the geodesic distance to the true spot, in metres, as measured by gis.tools (Haversine). **Anything under 10 m counts as d = 0.**
3. The top entry is worth a base of **1000 points**, modified by the multiplicative factors below.
4. Distance factor: **Urban θ = 2 / (1 + d/100)**, **non-Urban θ = 2 / (1 + d/10000)**.
5. Each step down the list multiplies by **0.95**: rank R starts at 1000 × 0.95^(R−1).
6. **Cues** (text the team writes to the agent, ≤ 1000 characters each) cost **×0.8** each. They must not name or hint at any place, directly or indirectly. The PDF's example of a banned indirect hint: "the most famous sport here is big-wave surfing" (meaning Nazaré).
7. **Multiple-choice questions** generated by the agent (≤ 4 options; about features, "geographic zones", etc.) cost **×0.95** each when answered, whether the answer is right or not. The options must come from the agent, not be suggested by the team.
8. If the agent returns a place name instead of coordinates (e.g. "Praça do Comércio"), the team may pick a coordinate inside that area at **×0.5**.
9. The factors from 6, 7 and 8 apply to **every candidate produced after them**, for that panorama only.
10. The **"Special One"** panorama, identified during the scoring run, is worth **×3**.
11. A panorama's score is the **sum** over all its candidates.
12. The team's score is the sum over panoramas. **Ties go to the team that finished faster.**

Repeating the same location several times in the list is explicitly allowed.

## Interaction rules

- During the scoring run the team may use any tools **on other equipment**, but may feed the agent **only text and multiple-choice clicks**.
- **Every interaction with the agent must be logged in JSON.** Send sample logs to the organisers on Discord to confirm the format.

## Partner clarifications (2026-10-10)

> 1 - It is not acceptable to use one panorama to help locate another panorama. The "agent" or "companion" must look at each panorama on its own.
>
> 2 - If the human decides not to answer the "agent" or "companion" question then there is no "cost" associated. Nevertheless, it is not fair to teach the agent to make questions where "no reply" is a meaningful answer.

What this means for our code:

- **Each panorama runs from a clean state:**
  - a new model conversation
  - an empty evidence file
  - no candidates, answers or downloaded galleries carried over from another panorama
- **No cross-panorama priors.** Same-day filenames suggest photos taken near each other, but the agent must not use that.
- **Warm-up and trial panoramas, and our hand labels for them, are for evaluation only.** They never go into the retrieval bank, the region classifier's training data or the knowledge base.
- **Data prepared in advance is fine:** model weights, the OSV-5M bank, the grid map, the knowledge base. None of it comes from challenge panoramas.
- **A skipped question carries no information.** The agent's beliefs stay exactly as they were, the log records the question as skipped, and the penalty tracker counts **answered questions only**.
- **Never write a question where silence means something.** No "skip if…" wording, and a skip must never be read as "no" or "none of these". If "none of these" is useful, it has to be one of the (at most 4) explicit options.

## Organiser answers (2026-10-10, evening)

Answers to the questions in PLAN.md section 8, relayed by the team:

| Question | Answer | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Is there a required JSON log schema? | No. What matters is that they can understand ours. | Keep `log.json` self-describing (`panoloc/runlog.py`): summaries first, then every event. |
| Do panoramas come with Urban/non-Urban labels? | Yes, but we only learn the label after our submission. | While solving, the category is unknown: score both scenarios (PLAN.md 1.3). The revealed label is for evaluating afterwards. |
| May the agent fetch external data during a run? | Yes. | Runtime OSM, Mapillary and image-search lookups are allowed. Keep timeouts and an offline fallback, because the venue network is unreliable. |
| What does a submission return? | A distance. | With the label revealed afterwards, a trial run's actual score can be computed. Never use one panorama's distance for another (partner clarification 1). |
| Are filename timestamps UTC or local time? | UTC. The summer period is UTC+1. | Read filename times as UTC. Portuguese summer time is UTC+1; mainland Spain and France use UTC+2. |
| Which list counts when outputs come both before and after an answered question? | "Doesn't really matter." | Keep a penalty snapshot with every output anyway. |
| Are chained zone questions OK, each with an explicit "none of these" or "outside" option? | Yes. | The zone fallback of PLAN.md 3.4 is allowed as designed. |
| Do the Sunday panoramas arrive together? | Not sure yet. | Build the panorama queue (TASKS.md T49) to work either way. |
